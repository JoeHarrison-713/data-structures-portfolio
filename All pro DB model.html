DATA SCIENCE PORTFOLIO PROJECT 2¶

Context: The purpose of this model is to see if we can evaluate defenders specifically defensive backs on an objective level from stats to see if we can determine who is an all pro. We want to determine this to decide who is truly the best of the best, this would help a team owner or General manager in real life to determine for example in a contract year how well someone is truly playing and what is their market worth. Do the numbers say you are an all pro or are you just living off of name recognition. Some people may not get the all pro know but still be playing at an all pro level. Theres 32 teams with multiple corners on safeties, however we only get 8-9 all pros per year. This is a classification problem because our model sets out to predict wether a DB is an All pro or not

Data Description and Prep: I was able to pull some official NFL stats from pythons nfl module and All pro dataset that was provided for me that I had to cross check from the NFLs official all pro list online. In order to clean the data I had to get rid of all the NFL players that were not Corners or Safeties, then I formatted the position groups to make sure it just had safeties and corners instead of things such as Free Safety, Strong Safety, Nickel corner, etc. Once that was complete I had to combine my stats I got from the official nfl python module and the all pro dataset before I ran any models on my data. I used one hot encoding on my all pro category to split it into all pro or not the difference between first and second team wasnt necessary here and made it easier to run my model to just group the category into all pro or not

Model Interpretation: From the model I was able to conclude that Safeties specifically with more interceptions tackles and pass deflections are more likely to be classified as "All pro". Our FI plots were able to show that interceptions and passes defended were the most influential stats, showing us that "splash plays" or "highlights" for safeties at least were important. Our confusion matrix shows that the model can correctly identify what isnt an all pro caliber player, but struggles to find true all pros due to the imbalance of the class, so many safeties in the league and only 3-4 all pro safeties every year. For our corners we were not able to predict true all pros due to the lack of coverage metrics available to us. All pro corners dont tend to make "splash plays" or highlights which I explain more in my limitations. These advanced coverage methods I believe would help us further refine our model and get a better hit on our corners as well as safeties.

Data Ethics and Limitations: Corners and Safeties have fairly different jobs and are measured in how good they are in seperate ways. For this exact reason I decided to split my data and models to where we were testing for all pro safeties and all pro corners instead of just all pro DBs. One of the limitations that I ran into which I wouldnt really notice til I was doing my models on my new dataset was that I wasnt able to get any next gen stats for defensive backs, or any of the more important stats for a Corner such as completion percentage against, targets, expected completion percentage against, etc which really hinders my model when predicting an All pro corner. All pro corners may not necessarily have the flashy official stat numbers such as interceptions and tackles because their coverage ability limits targets, for example Christian Gonzales who was a second team all pro in 2024 but didnt necessarily have gaudy numbers, he had only 2 interceptions, with 10 pass deflections, but his ability to cover is what got him the nod which isnt necessarily a stat that you can put a whole number on.
